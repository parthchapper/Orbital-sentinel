UAE Swath —
The scan line is where the pushbroom imager is looking right now; the shaded band is everything it has already captured this pass. The instrument sees a 180 km swath at 30 m ground resolution, so one crossing covers the entire UAE coast in a single strip.
Electrical Power —
0.10 m² of triple-junction cells make ~41 W in full sun. The bus draws 6.2 W always; the imager 22 W; the X-band radio 9 W. Over a 35-minute eclipse that is only a 5 % depth of discharge — which is what a real orbit looks like. The gauge is therefore zoomed to the mission's actual range, and the true percentage is printed beside it.

Spectroscopy — HYPER-A 96 bands · 400–900 nm
Each bar is one spectral band of reflected light. Algae leave a signature: chlorophyll-a absorbs blue (443 nm) and red (665 nm), reflects green (555 nm), and — the decisive one — re-emits light at 681 nm. That fluorescence peak only appears over living algae, which is why this is worth measuring from orbit rather than inferring from water colour.
Desalination Intake Schedule —
Algal blooms clog reverse-osmosis intakes — the 2008–09 bloom in the Gulf of Oman repeatedly shut Fujairah down. Operators find out when intake pressure drops. This converts the orbital measurement into the only thing they can act on: when to draw water. Hover a bar to see exactly which terms drove that hour's score.
Live Downlink —
Raw telemetry as the ground station receives it, tagged with CCSDS application IDs. Every number here is the same number shown on the gauges — nothing in this log is filler. The line rate rises with activity: one per tick in eclipse, four during a pass.
